    Handbook

    Run a Hong Kong store for 30 days with 270 starting boxes. Each morning, receive shipments from Japan, choose an order or zero, then finish the day to reveal demand. Demand is uniformly distributed from 20 to 80 boxes a day.

    Delivery takes 3 days: order on Day 1, receive before sales on Day 4. Balance missed sales against storage and ordering costs. Count stock already in transit before ordering again.

    Buy one boxHK$400
    Sell one boxHK$550
    Store one closing-stock boxHK$2 / night
    Place one nonzero orderHK$800 / order
    Current order commitment

    The selected order commits HK$0 to products. Product purchases become inventory assets, not immediate profit expenses. The order fee is a separate expense.

    Profit is not cash flow

    Each day's profit is sales revenue minus the cost of the boxes sold, the overnight storage bill, and the supplier ordering fee. Buying stock creates an inventory asset; only sold boxes become cost of goods sold.

    This simplified game has no cash limit. Unsold boxes and incoming shipments retain their purchase-cost value, but are not sales or bonus profit. You still pay storage on Day 30. Orders arriving after Day 30 cannot serve this run, and their ordering fee still counts.

    What is fill rate?

    The share of all requested boxes you actually sold. Selling 90 of 100 requested boxes gives 90%. Missed demand leaves permanently; there is no waiting list or extra stockout fine.

    Average daily profit is your total profit divided by completed days. Fill rate uses total boxes, not an average of daily percentages.

    The EOQ store: a fair reference, not a perfect oracle

    Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) balances ordering fees against storage costs. Here it suggests batches of 200 boxes.

    Q = sqrt(2 × 50 boxes/day × HK$800/order ÷ HK$2/box/day) = 200 boxes

    EOQ tells you how much to order, not when. Our preset store checks each morning and orders 200 boxes when stock plus boxes in transit is 210 or less.

    That trigger is 150 boxes of expected demand during the 3-day lead time plus a fixed 60-box safety buffer. The buffer is a teaching assumption, not a guarantee. The EOQ store does not order after Day 27 or look ahead at tomorrow's demand.

    You and the EOQ store start with the same 270 boxes and face identical seeded demand (Mulberry32, seed 42). It is an EOQ-based policy, not a claim of optimal inventory control.

    How your combined rating works

    We calculate your relative shortfall from EOQ for profit and fill rate, then use the larger shortfall. An improvement in one cannot cancel a shortfall in the other.

    • Excellent: both within 5% of EOQ or better.
    • Almost successful: the larger shortfall is over 5% and up to 20%.
    • Refine your strategy: either shortfall is over 20%.

    Shortfalls are relative percentages, not percentage-point gaps. We use unrounded values for the rating and count superior performance as zero shortfall.

    The simplified world behind the game

    Demand is independently sampled as a whole number from 20 to 80, inclusive. Supplier availability is unlimited. There are no capacity constraints, returns, tax, spoilage, or backorders. One order is allowed per day. All prices and fees are fictional educational assumptions.

    Replays use the same demand sequence so you can compare strategies. A browser-only game cannot conceal its source code or future demand from someone inspecting the file; play without looking ahead for a meaningful challenge.
